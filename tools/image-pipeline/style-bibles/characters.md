# Recurring-character system

This cast makes people in otherwise different curriculum scenes feel like they belong to one Northern Nigerian primary-school world. It is a continuity guide, not a requirement: object-only and diagram-only images use no character unless a learner helps explain the concept.

## Shared rendering rules

Children have believable child proportions: heads are slightly larger than adults', never caricatured; features are individually varied but restrained; expressions are readable at mobile-card size. Skin is painted with warm umber, deep brown, and golden-brown ranges using natural undertones, never a single default tone. Clothes, rooms, homes, farms, markets, foods, tools, and vegetation must be specific to the scene and culturally plausible for Northern Nigeria without turning any person or setting into decoration.

## Continuity boundaries

- Keep each named child’s relative height, skin-tone family, hair/covering logic, and core palette consistent across uses.
- Do not make all girls wear identical coverings, all boys wear identical caps, or imply a uniform religious identity. Clothing follows the particular scene, family, school, weather, and setting.
- Do not age characters up or down, alter their ethnicity, give them exaggerated eyes/lips/noses, make them fashion models, or depict unsafe handling merely to make an image exciting.
- Do not force the cast into a lesson: pure plant, object, science-process, or mathematics-diagram images should remain uncluttered.
